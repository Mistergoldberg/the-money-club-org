Product Assortment Analysis

A Money Club worksheet for turning a parent problem into shelf evidence and a testable product hypothesis.

Pairs with

Market Research: Follow the Money & Problems

Goal

Scan a category, count the patterns, and decide what product might be worth testing locally.

Big question

Does the shelf evidence support the parent problem, or is the idea still just a guess?

Interactive Worksheet

Overview

A parent survey gives clues. A store shelf gives evidence.

This worksheet helps you slow down before building. Start with the parent problem, scan the product category, count the patterns, and write a testable hypothesis.

SKU count does not equal sales.

Good market research collects signals before making decisions.

Start With the Survey

Use the parent problem from the previous module. The goal is to choose one category worth scanning.

Scan the Shelf

Collect product names, sizes, prices, brands, pack counts, and review signals. AI can help gather and sort the information, but you still decide what the pattern means.

SKU Pattern Log

A SKU is one specific product listing. Record a few examples, then look for repeats.

Reviews and Friction
