The Money Club.Org

A Toronto nonprofit maker camp that teaches financial literacy, design thinking, and public speaking.

The Maker Market

The program ends with a live Maker Market.

Every team sets up their idea and pitches it in a market-style format.

Some students present products.

Some present services.

Some present apps or community ideas.

Each participant gets virtual cash to spend on the ideas they think are strongest.

That means every team has to answer a real question:

Why will someone choose one idea over another?

Build & Sell

Babysitting. Tutoring. Swim lessons. Paper routes.

Those are real ways kids start earning.

The Money Club takes that idea further.

Instead of working alone for an hourly rate, students work in teams with a project budget to build something bigger - a product, a service, or a simple community app people might actually choose, use, and pay for.

Think:

  • a smarter lemonade stand
  • a neighborhood subscription service
  • a small business idea that solves a real problem

Over four weeks, students learn how to spot demand, shape an offer, test pricing, gather feedback, and improve their idea in the real world.

How the 4 Weeks Work

Week 1 — Learn the System

Students build a foundation in money, pricing, wages, margins, and market research so they can make better decisions in the weeks ahead.

Week 2 — Build a Product Concept

Students study a real product category, develop their own version, and learn how pricing, packaging, and positioning work together.

Week 3 — Create and Test an Idea

Students build their own product or service idea, talk to potential users, and prepare it for the Maker Market.

Week 4 — Maker Market + Final Showcase

Students present their products at the Maker Market, where everyone gets virtual cash to invest in the ideas they believe in, then refine their thinking and present what they built, why it works, and what they would do next.

Where the Program Happens

The Money Club runs at the UTSU Student Commons, 230 College Street, near the University of Toronto.

Students learn in a supervised classroom setting with access to breakout space, lounge seating, and a lunch area. The location is also steps from the TTC, making commuting easier for families.
