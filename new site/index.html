Where the Program Happens

The Money Club runs at the UTSU Student Commons, located at 230 College Street in downtown Toronto, near the University of Toronto.

The location is convenient for families across central Toronto, including the Annex, Kensington Market, Chinatown, and surrounding downtown neighborhoods.

Students meet in a supervised classroom environment where they develop real projects, collaborate with peers, and present their final ideas.

Light daily snacks are provided. Students should bring their own lunch and water bottle.

Built for learning — not profit

The Money Club.Org is a community-first program.

It is not designed to maximize profit. It is designed to teach financial literacy by example.

That's why we run the program using open-book financials — so families can see exactly where tuition goes, and students can learn by watching money move through a real system.

How the 4 Weeks Work

Each week builds on the last. Students move from understanding how money and businesses work → to designing products → to building and testing their own ideas.

  • Week 1 — Decision Bootcamp

    Students learn how money and work actually function in the real world—reading simple agreements, modeling wages and costs, breaking down how businesses survive, and running their first market research field study so they can build ideas based on evidence, not guesswork.

  • Week 2 — Control Brand Sprint

    Students reverse-engineer a real product category (like garbage bags or snacks), studying competitors, designing their own “control brand,” and learning how packaging, pricing, sourcing, and positioning combine to create a product that can actually compete.

  • Week 3 — Marketplace Build

    Students identify a real customer problem, validate the idea through interviews and quick research, then prototype, cost, and price their own product or service while preparing a launch plan for a simulated marketplace.

  • Week 4 — Scale + Showcase

    Students refine their product based on feedback, improve the economics and story behind it, and present their work at the final showcase—explaining not just what they built, but why the idea works and what they would do next.

Is This a STEM Program?

It works alongside STEM — but focuses on the layer most STEM programs skip.

Many STEM camps teach students how to code, build robots, or engineer projects.

The Money Club asks a different question:

What should you build — and why would anyone want it?

Students learn to observe how people interact with products and systems, identify real problems, and test ideas through design thinking.

That might include:

  • designing a product or service
  • prototyping an idea
  • building a simple digital tool
  • interviewing potential users
  • testing pricing and demand
  • developing branding, photography, or marketing materials
  • presenting the idea clearly to others

For students who already enjoy coding, robotics, or making things, The Money Club provides the real-world context those skills often lack.

It’s not just about building something.

It’s about building something that solves a real problem — and understanding the systems that make it work.
